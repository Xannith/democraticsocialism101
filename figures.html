Who Identifies as a Democratic Socialist

This page lists only public figures who publicly self-identify as democratic socialists, cited to their own statements or to documented membership in the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA). We do not include anyone on the basis of a label applied by others, whether by supporters or by opponents. We abbreviate democratic socialism as DemSoc after this first mention. Self-identification and affiliation can change, so this list is deliberately short and conservative, and we expand it only when we can cite a person's own words or documented DSA membership.

Last verified: 2026-07-22

Bernie Sanders

United States Senator from Vermont. Sanders has publicly identified as a democratic socialist for decades. He is not a member of the DSA. His policy program is often described by analysts as closer to social democracy, a point discussed on the taxonomy page.

Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez

United States Representative from New York. Ocasio-Cortez is a self-identified democratic socialist, which is the basis for her inclusion here. She won her 2018 congressional primary as a DSA-endorsed candidate, though the DSA withdrew its national endorsement of her in 2024, so her current relationship to the organization is contested.

Zohran Mamdani

Mayor of New York City. Mamdani is a member of the DSA and was sworn in as mayor on January 1, 2026. At his inauguration he stated, "I was elected as a democratic socialist, and I will govern as a democratic socialist."

Rashida Tlaib

United States Representative from Michigan. Tlaib is DSA-aligned. We note her basis for inclusion as alignment rather than a formal statement of self-identification or documented membership, and we will refine this entry as the primary source is confirmed.

A note on this list

Officeholders, memberships, and affiliations change over time. We review this page periodically and update the Last verified date above when we do. Additions are made only after we can cite a figure's own statement or documented DSA membership, never on the basis of labels applied by others. For the policies these figures advocate, see the United States case study.
